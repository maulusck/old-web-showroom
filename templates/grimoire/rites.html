The Index of Rites

Herein are catalogued the workings of the Codex, ranked not by power but by peril. A number in the margin marks the leaf on which each is set down in full. Where a rite bears no number, it was struck from the fair copy and survives only in whisper. Read across before you read down, and let no apprentice attempt a rite above the third until a lamplighter has stood watch beside them.

The elder folio ordered these differently. We have set them as the candle burns them — slowest first, so the hand is steady before the toll comes due.

I · The Kindling of the Lesser Flame

Leaf the first. A threshold working, safe for the untried hand. One candle, one pinch of autumn salt, one name surrendered. The whole Order learns it before any other.

II · On Letters That Arrive Unsent

Leaf the ninth. Not a rite to perform but one to endure. Read it before the ink does, and answer nothing until the sun is up and the page has cooled.

III · The Tincture of Quiet Waters

Leaf the fourteenth. Nine nights of moonwort under a covered jar. A thimble forgets one true thing; a cup forgets the one who drank. Measure with care.

— · To Read a Sky Gone Strange

Leaf unnumbered, struck from the copy. Spoken of only when an eighth star joins the Lantern's seven. Shutter the window. Count the visitor's patience, not its coming.

On the Numbering of Leaves

A rite set down in full bears a Roman numeral and the leaf that holds it. Follow the number to the working; do not work from this index alone, for the index keeps the names but not the measures, and a rite half-remembered is a rite that collects its toll twice. The only working here copied entire is the first — begin there, and return to it often, for the Lesser Flame is the lock to which every further leaf is a key.
