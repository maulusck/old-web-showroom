Upon the Reading of Shadows

Herein lie the collected workings of our sisterhood and brotherhood of lamplighters, copied faithfully from the water-stained leaves of the elder folio. Read slowly, and never after the third bell. What is inked here was once whispered, and what is whispered is never wholly forgotten. Let the uninitiated close the cover; let the curious keep a candle near.

Note, in a later hand: three pages were cut from this chapter with a clean blade. The librarian denies all knowledge of the matter.

The Kindling of the Lesser Flame

A working for the long dark of the year. Requires one honest candle, a pinch of salt kept dry since autumn, and a name you are willing to let go of.

On Letters That Arrive Unsent

Should ink appear upon a page you did not write, do not answer before dawn. The Order has lost two scribes to premature correspondence.

The Tincture of Quiet Waters

Steep moonwort beneath a covered jar for nine nights. Drink but a thimble. It grants no visions — only the grace to forget one true thing.

To Read a Sky Gone Strange

When an eighth star joins the seven of the Lantern, draw the shutters and keep counsel. It is not an omen. It is a visitor, and it is patient.

A Word Before the Workings

Every rite copied into this codex has been tested by lamplight and lost sleep alike, and every one carries its toll. We do not sell certainty; we lend the shape of it. If a charm fails, suspect first your patience, second your salt, and last — only last — the charm. Keep this book flat, its spine unbent, and your doubts to yourself until morning. The Veiled Lantern keeps its own hours, and so, in time, shall you.
