Leaf the First · Rite I

The Kindling of the Lesser Flame

Worked for the long dark of the year, when the sun keeps short hours and the house grows colder than its fires explain. This is the first rite the Order sets before a new hand, for it asks little and teaches much: that a flame is a bargain, and every bargain has its price named aloud. Perform it alone, after the second bell and before the third, with the shutters drawn and no mirror uncovered in the room.

What the Rite Requires

  • One honest candle of unbleached tallow, never before lit.
  • A pinch of salt kept dry since the last day of autumn.
  • A single hair, given freely and not taken.
  • A name you are willing to let go of, written small on the back of your hand.

The Working

Set the candle in a ring of the salt, no wider than your spread hand. Speak the words below once, plainly, as you would read a letter to a friend — not as a performance, for the flame knows the difference and will not answer a show. At the last line, lay the given hair across the wick and strike your light. Should the candle take, the name on your hand will fade by morning, and with it the thing you bound to it. Should it refuse, scatter the salt at once and do not try again until the next dark of the year.

The Incantation

Little light, I keep you small, / and small you keep the dark. / I give a name I need no more; / take it for your spark. / Burn until the morning bell, / ask nothing else of me, / and what I spoke into your flame / let the morning never see.

Warning, in the librarian's hand: never give your own name to the Lesser Flame, nor the name of any soul still living. A surrendered name does not return. Two apprentices learned this; we do not speak theirs, for there is nothing left to speak.

Afterward

Let the candle burn to nothing of its own accord — pinch it out and the bargain stands unfinished, and an unfinished bargain collects what it is owed by other means. When morning comes and the ring of salt is grey, sweep it into running water. Then return to the Index of Rites and set down, in the margin beside this leaf, the date and what it cost you. The Order keeps no ledger but the one each hand keeps honestly.
