Welcome to our little corner of the night sky!

Pour yourself some coffee, dim the lights, and let your eyes adjust. The Dark Sky Society is a club of amateur stargazers who meet on the first clear Friday of every month out at Culver's Ridge, far from the streetlights. We drag out the Dobsonians, swap eyepieces, argue about collimation, and point at faint smudges until dawn. No membership fee — just bring a red flashlight and a thermos. This page is hand-coded by our webmaster in Notepad, so mind the dust.

About the Society

We started in the spring of 1994 when four of us — Ed, Marge, Lorraine, and the fellow we only know as "Doc" — got tired of fighting the glow of the mall parking lot and drove out to Culver's Ridge with a borrowed 8-inch Dob. Word got around. These days we're about thirty regulars, from a retired machinist who grinds his own mirrors to a sixth-grader with a pair of binoculars and more patience than any of us.

The rules are short: no white light at the field after dark, pack out what you pack in, and anyone who spots something gets to say so loud enough for the rest of us to swing over and look. We meet the first clear Friday of each month. "Clear" is decided by the webmaster's kitchen window and a quick call to Ed.

The Star Log

Field notes from recent outings, typed up from the clipboard the morning after. Transparency and seeing are the webmaster's own guesses, so argue amongst yourselves.

Nov 7 — Culver's Ridge

Transparency 4/5, seeing poor. Chased the Double Cluster and M31 naked-eye before moonrise. Lorraine split the Double-Double at 180x once the air settled. Home by 1 AM, toes gone.

Oct 10 — Culver's Ridge

Best night of the fall. M33 showed spiral structure in the 12-inch. Doc swears he caught the Horsehead with an H-beta filter; the rest of us saw a smudge and a rumor.

Sep 12 — Reservoir Lot

Clouded out by 10, so we stayed in the cars and swapped eyepiece catalogs. Marge handed around her Hale-Bopp slides. Not a total loss.

Sky Charts

Printable finder charts drawn up by Ed in his garage. Print them on a laser printer, trim the margins, and read them under a red lamp so you keep your dark adaptation. Grab the files themselves over in Downloads.

  • Spring Galaxies — the Virgo Cluster and the Leo Triplet, naked-eye stars down to mag 5.
  • Summer Milky Way — Sagittarius to Cygnus, with the showpiece nebulae circled.
  • The Double Cluster Hop — a star-hop from Cassiopeia for binocular users.
  • Winter Showpieces — Orion, the Pleiades, and the big open clusters of Auriga.
