CHAPTER ONE — THE THREE O'CLOCK BOOTH

Dame in the Dark · by Vincent “Slug” Malory

The Last Cup of the Night

Three o'clock in the morning has a color, and it is the color of cold coffee under a dying bulb. Carla Reyes knew that color the way other women knew their own eyes in a mirror. She had wiped down the same twelve feet of counter for six years, and in six years nothing had ever walked through the door of the Blue Owl that a wet rag couldn't fix.

Then the man in the camel coat took the corner booth, and he didn't order anything, and he didn't take off his hat. He just watched the street the way a cat watches a crack in the wall — patient, certain, already hungry. Carla brought him water he didn't ask for. He slid an envelope under the sugar jar and said, low enough that the jukebox ate half of it, “If I don't come back for this, honey, you never saw it. And you never saw me.”

He came back for nothing. By the time the squad cars peeled the night open with their red mouths, the camel coat was a chalk outline on the sidewalk and the envelope was a hot coal burning a hole in Carla's apron. She had sixty cents in tips, a borrowed pistol she didn't know how to use, and a name she hadn't heard spoken aloud in years whispering up at her from the paper inside. Her own.
