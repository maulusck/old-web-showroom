GREAT BRIDGE OPENS AT LAST; WHOLE CITY POURS ACROSS

Nine Years In The Building, The Iron Span Over The Copper River Is Thrown Open To Foot And Motor — Mayor Snips Ribbon With Borrowed Shears As Brass Band Plays And A Prize Hog Leads The Parade.

By Our Special Correspondent — Telegraphed From The Far Bank

THEY said it could not be done, and for the better part of a decade they were, to all appearances, entirely correct. Yet yesterday at the stroke of noon the great iron bridge that has loomed half-finished over the Copper River since the spring floods of ’17 stood complete, painted, and open to any citizen with the nerve to cross it.

By half past the hour the span was black with people. Clerks came in their collars, farmers in their boots, and no fewer than three brass bands, none of which had agreed beforehand upon a tune. The effect, one onlooker remarked, was “grand in the general and dreadful in the particular.”

The Mayor, arriving by motor-car some minutes behind his own schedule, delivered remarks of which little was audible over the wind and the hogs. He is understood to have thanked the engineers, the river, and Providence, in that order, before cutting the ceremonial ribbon with a pair of tailor’s shears hastily lent by a draper on the quay.

Engineers of the works pronounced the structure sound “for a hundred years and a week besides,” a figure this paper is in no position to verify and in no hurry to test. The first motor-car across was a cream touring model belonging to Alderman Pruett, who reversed halfway over, lost his nerve, and had to be waved onward by a constable.

Tolls To Begin Monday Next

The City Works Committee advises that crossings shall be free of charge through the weekend, after which a toll of one cent per pedestrian and three per motor will be levied, children and funeral processions excepted. Season passes may be had at the Works Office on application and the usual argument.

Not all received the news gladly. The ferrymen of the Lower Landing, whose trade the bridge is expected to sink entirely, gathered in silent protest upon the far bank and were observed to be charging double for the last crossings of the old order.

WEATHER

To-day: Fair and bright, a stiff westerly off the river, turning brisk by evening. To-morrow: Cloud with the chance of a passing shower. Barometer steady. Mariners advised to mind the current beneath the new span.

A Word From The Editor

This journal has, these nine years, printed every rumour of the bridge’s collapse, abandonment, and haunting. We are pleased to retract them all at once and to observe that a thing long promised has, against the run of experience, actually arrived. Readers are reminded that the old ferry timetable, long a fixture of this page, will be discontinued and may be used to light the stove.
