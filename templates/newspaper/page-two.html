…AND THE FAR BANK DISCOVERS IT HAS NEIGHBOURS

The Span Open A Single Day, The Two Halves Of The Town Set About The Delicate Business Of Making One Another’s Acquaintance — With Mixed Results At The Dinner Hour.

By Our Special Correspondent — Continued From The Front Page

BY the second morning the novelty of the crossing had not worn thin so much as changed its character. Where Thursday brought brass bands and prize hogs, Friday brought errands: a baker’s cart, a doctor’s gig, and a long file of the merely curious come to see what the far bank had been hiding these forty years.

What it had been hiding, chiefly, was another town very like this one, convinced of its own superiority on grounds no one on the near bank had ever thought to dispute. The introduction was cordial. The dinner that followed was not.

At the Ferryman’s Arms, now ferryless and casting about for a new name, the first joint custom from across the water called for its beef well done and was served it rare, an insult the proprietor insists was culinary and the guest maintains was political. The matter was settled, as such matters are, by a second round and a general agreement to forget it.

The Ferrymen Reconsidered

This paper reported yesterday that the ferrymen of the Lower Landing had gathered in silent protest. They have since found their voices. A deputation of four, led by the elder Mr. Hale, called upon the Works Office to propose that the ferry be preserved “for sentiment, and for the days the bridge is shut,” the bridge having been shut precisely once, for ten minutes, while a gull was removed from the machinery.

The Committee received them warmly and promised nothing, which both parties understood to be the whole of the transaction. Mr. Hale is said to be weighing a career in haulage.

Council Counts The Cost

The final reckoning of the works, long a secret kept by arithmetic, was laid before the Council on Wednesday and found to exceed the original estimate by a sum the Treasurer described as “regrettable but historic.” The overage, he explained, owed to the flood of ’17, the strike of ’21, the paint of ’24, and a quantity of rivets that appear simply to have walked off.

An inquiry was proposed, debated, and adjourned until such time as the bridge should either pay for itself or fall down, whichever should first relieve the Council of the question.

Lost And Found

The prize hog that led Thursday’s parade has not been seen since it crossed the river and is presumed to have emigrated. A reward is offered by its owner, who adds that it answers to “Alderman” and has opinions. Finders may apply at the Works Office, where a queue is already forming for other reasons entirely.
